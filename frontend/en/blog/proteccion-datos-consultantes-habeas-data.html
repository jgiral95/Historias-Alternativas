Protecting your clients' data in Colombia (Habeas Data)

What a practitioner should keep in mind when storing clients' personal data under Law 1581 of 2012. General information, not legal advice.

When you record your clients' names, contact details and health data, you are processing personal data. In Colombia the Habeas Data regime applies, mainly Law 1581 of 2012. This article is informational and does not replace a lawyer's advice.

Key ideas

  • Prior authorization: the client must know what data you collect and what you use it for, and give their authorization before processing.
  • Sensitive data: health information is sensitive; the client isn't obliged to provide it and you must handle it with special care.
  • Purpose: use the data only for the support you promised, not for advertising or other purposes.
  • Rights of the data subject: to access, update, rectify and request deletion of their data, or revoke the authorization.
  • Security: adopt measures to prevent loss, unauthorized access or use.

Good practices

  1. Have a visible privacy policy and a channel for requests.
  2. Ask for the authorization in writing together with the consent.
  3. Keep files on a platform with protected, encrypted access, not in chats or loose sheets.
  4. Limit who has access: nobody on your team should see more than necessary.
  5. Define how long you keep the data and how you delete it.

When you use a platform

You remain responsible toward your clients. Check that the platform explains how it processes and protects data. In Alter-Karute you can read the privacy policy.

For your specific case, consult a lawyer or the Superintendencia de Industria y Comercio.
